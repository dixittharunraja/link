# Turnitin assumptions: evidence and limits

The request names perplexity, predictability, sentence-length variation and passive voice, but does not contain a separate detailed explanation to audit. The assessment below therefore addresses those named propositions only.

| Proposition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| The 26% indicator is a 26% probability that the authors used AI. | Incorrect interpretation. It describes the portion of qualifying prose classified by the system, not an authorship probability. It is separate from a similarity percentage. [Turnitin report guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report). |
| Highlighted sentences prove AI authorship. | Unsupported. Turnitin acknowledges misclassification and requires human judgment; its report is not sufficient on its own for adverse action. [Turnitin report guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report). |
| Turnitin mainly applies explicit perplexity and burstiness rules. | The August 2023 Turnitin-authored whitepaper describes a trained transformer and explains why simple hand-curated measures are insufficient. Perplexity and burstiness are discussed as background concepts, not disclosed as a complete decision rule. [Turnitin whitepaper, sections on detection research and solution overview; hosted mirror](https://device.report/m/c6710dd00da4c260eb62220557b0cb6a91389e74da7e3ea9666c9a159e52e447). |
| Predictable wording can affect a statistical detector. | Plausible at a general level: the whitepaper discusses learned statistical patterns and context windows. It does not reveal why a particular sentence in this report was flagged or how individual words are weighted. [Turnitin whitepaper](https://device.report/m/c6710dd00da4c260eb62220557b0cb6a91389e74da7e3ea9666c9a159e52e447). |
| Varying sentence lengths will reliably lower the score. | Not established by the cited documentation. The historical architecture description does not supply a sentence-length recipe or a guaranteed score response. Natural sentence structure is an editorial choice, not an optimization target. [Turnitin whitepaper](https://device.report/m/c6710dd00da4c260eb62220557b0cb6a91389e74da7e3ea9666c9a159e52e447). |
| Passive voice is a documented detection trigger. | No support found in the official materials reviewed. That is not proof the model can never respond to syntactic patterns; it means there is no documented basis for the proposed rule. Use active or passive voice according to the technical emphasis. |
| Abstracts and conclusions are inherently AI-like. | Unsupported as a general rule. Turnitin reported elevated false positives near document boundaries and a mitigation in May 2023. That historical observation cannot identify the cause of this report; this paper’s conclusion has no visible highlight. [Turnitin model release notes](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/28294949544717-AI-writing-detection-model). |
| The 2023 whitepaper fully specifies the detector used in September 2026. | Unsupported. Later model updates are documented, including February 2026. Historical architecture is evidence about the disclosed design, not a complete specification of the current classifier. [Turnitin model release notes](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/28294949544717-AI-writing-detection-model). |

The edits use the report to locate passages for review. They remove generic framing, clarify operations and scope claims to their evidence. They do not use a humanizer, introduce errors, manipulate sentence lengths, or promise 0% (or any other score). No new detector evaluation was performed.
